# Niche Selection

Build 3 candidates. Check that money already moves. Score. Pick one.

## Step 1: Build 3 candidates

Each candidate = one business type + one concrete task from their skill + their edge.

Format: "[Task] for [specific business type] because [edge]"

Good: "Missed call text back setup for independent physio clinics in the UK, because of 2 years at a physio front desk."
Bad: "AI automation for healthcare." (too wide, no edge, no task)

Specific means narrow enough that a list of 200 to 5,000 businesses can be pulled. "Small businesses" fails. "Roofing contractors in Texas with 5 to 30 staff" passes.

## Step 2: Money check (use web search)

For each candidate, look for at least two signs that businesses already pay to solve this:

| Signal | Where to look |
|---|---|
| Job posts for the task | LinkedIn Jobs, Indeed, Upwork job feed ("[task] [industry]") |
| Freelancers or agencies already selling it | Google "[task] for [industry]", agency pricing pages, Fiverr gigs with sales |
| Software built for this niche's problem | G2, Capterra category pages, "[industry] software" |
| Complaints about the problem | Reddit (r/smallbusiness, industry subs), G2 low reviews, Facebook groups |
| Ad spend in the niche | Meta Ad Library search for the business type |

Also grab a price range from what you find. It feeds the offer.

No money signal = drop the candidate.

## Step 3: Score each (1 to 5)

1. **Trust speed:** how fast will this buyer trust this beginner, given the edge?
2. **Pain:** is the problem costing them money they can see?
3. **Can pay:** do these businesses have budget for a service at this price?
4. **Findable:** can a list be built with filters and public data?
5. **Fit:** can the beginner deliver it with the skill they have today?

Pick the highest total. Ties go to **Findable**, then **Trust speed**. Beginners win on reachable markets where they're believable, not on the biggest market.

## Beginner friendly niche traits

- Owner is the buyer (no committees)
- Pain is visible from outside (bad reviews, slow site, no ads, missed calls)
- Many businesses of the same type
- Revenue per customer is high enough to pay for help (clinics, contractors, law firms, ecommerce brands, coaches with programs)

## Avoid for beginners

- Enterprise buyers and long sales cycles
- Niches where the buyer can't afford the price
- Regulated work the beginner isn't qualified for (legal filings, medical advice, financial advice)
- Niches picked only because they're trendy with no link to the edge
