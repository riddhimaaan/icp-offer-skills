# ICP Engine

Use this reference during Phase 1.

Inputs:
- Product or service.
- Target audience.
- Proof status.

Create an ICP psychographics document. Keep it grounded in the user's inputs. When a detail is inferred, write it as likely, not certain. When using a number that did not come from the user, label it "(estimate)".

## Output Rules

- Use simple, real words.
- Make every line specific to this buyer.
- Do not use generic business filler.
- Do not invent exact quotes, exact past attempts, exact costs, or exact metrics as facts.
- Use buyer-language quotes only as plausible examples unless the user gave real quotes.

## ICP Psychographics Document

Produce this exact structure.

### Offer

One sentence: what it is, who it is for, and what it does.

### Who They Are

Two or three sentences describing the buyer's job, work world, and usual day.

### Section 1: Job Titles and What They Actually Do

**Job Titles**

List 3-5 common titles for this audience.

**What their day actually looks like**

- List real tasks, meetings, and decisions they deal with.
- Use specific situations.
- Avoid broad labels like "manages team" unless you explain the actual work.

**What they are responsible for**

- What their boss, board, clients, or team holds them to.
- What numbers or outcomes they need to show.
- What they get measured on.

### Section 2: Problems and Frustrations

List 3-5 problems. Use this format for each one:

**Problem [number]: [Name of the problem]**

- **What they might say out loud:** A plausible first-person complaint. Use quotation marks. If the user gave a real quote, mark it as a real quote.
- **What is actually going on:** The likely cause, not just the symptom.
- **What happens if it does not get fixed:** The specific cost in lost money, lost time, lost trust, missed work, or stress. Use estimates only when needed.
- **How bad it feels:** Low / Medium / High, plus one sentence explaining why.

### Section 3: What They Want

**The specific results they are chasing**

List 3-5 concrete results. Use numbers only when the user gave them or when you label them as estimates.

**How they know something is working**

- What they check.
- What they measure.
- What signal makes them believe the fix is working.

**What their life looks like when the problem is solved**

Give a short, specific picture of what changes in their work, time, stress, or reputation.

### Section 4: What They Really Want

**Who they want to be**

- From: one honest sentence about how they likely feel now.
- To: one honest sentence about how they want to feel.

**How they want people to see them**

- What they want their team, clients, peers, boss, investors, or family to think.
- What they would want someone to say about them.

**What they are scared of**

- What they worry will happen if they try something new and it does not work.
- What they think it says about them if they still cannot fix the problem.
- What they may have tried before. Mark this as likely unless the user gave specifics.

**The real reason behind every result they want**

For each result from Section 3, write the emotion under it.

Example:
- "They say they want 5 more sales calls a week. What they likely want is proof that demand is real."
