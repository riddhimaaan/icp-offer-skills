# PMF Synthesis Engine

Use this reference during Phase 2 and when printing the PMF report.

Inputs:
- Product or service description.
- Proof status.
- ICP document.

Score how well the product fits the ICP across four dimensions.

Write the report so a busy, non-expert reader can understand it on the first pass.

Rules:
- Say the answer first.
- Use plain labels like "Good," "Okay," "Bad," and "Fix this."
- Explain what each score means in normal words.
- Turn every weakness into a next step.
- Avoid theory unless it changes what the user should do next.
- Print only the score, plain-English answer, and what to fix.

Scoring:
- Strong Fit = 2.5 points
- Partial Fit = 1.25 points
- Gap = 0 points

Labels:
- 8.5-10: Ready
- 6-8: Mostly Ready
- 3-5.5: Needs Work
- 0-2.5: Misaligned

## Dimension 1: Problem-Solution Fit

Compare:
- ICP Section 2: top problems in the buyer's words.
- Product: what the product actually does.

Check:
1. Does the product directly solve the top painful problem?
2. Does the product use language that matches how the buyer describes the problem?
3. Does the product address the consequence of not fixing it?
4. Or does it solve a lower-priority problem?

Ratings:
- **Strong Fit:** Solves the top 1-2 problems directly.
- **Partial Fit:** Helps the right area but misses the sharpest pain.
- **Gap:** Solves a weak problem or a problem the ICP does not clearly have.

## Dimension 2: Desire-Outcome Fit

Compare:
- ICP Section 3: results, success signals, and after-state.
- Product: outcome the product delivers.

Check:
1. Does the product deliver the result the buyer wants?
2. Does success look the same to the buyer and the product?
3. Does the after-state match?
4. Is the timeframe believable?

Ratings:
- **Strong Fit:** Outcome and success metric match the buyer's desired result.
- **Partial Fit:** Outcome is related but not exact.
- **Gap:** Product promises something the buyer does not rank highly.

## Dimension 3: Identity-Positioning Fit

Compare:
- ICP Section 4: who they want to become and how they want to be seen.
- Product: how the product is described and what buyer it implies.

Check:
1. Does using this product help the buyer feel like the person they want to be?
2. Does the implied buyer match their desired identity?
3. Would buying this make them look good to the people they care about?
4. Or does it feel too junior, too risky, or too generic?

Ratings:
- **Strong Fit:** Product reinforces the identity the buyer wants.
- **Partial Fit:** Product is useful but does not speak to identity.
- **Gap:** Product conflicts with the buyer's desired identity or ignores it.

## Dimension 4: Fear-Objection Fit

Compare:
- ICP Section 4: fears, past failures, and risk.
- Product: proof points, guarantees, mechanism, and differentiation.

Check:
1. Does the product address fear of trying and failing?
2. Does it answer specific past failures?
3. Does proof speak to the buyer's skepticism?
4. Or are the biggest fears still open?

Ratings:
- **Strong Fit:** Specific proof or mechanism answers the buyer's real fears.
- **Partial Fit:** Some proof exists, but emotional risk remains.
- **Gap:** No clear proof, guarantee, or mechanism answers the fear.

## Simplified PMF Report Format

```markdown
# PMF Report: [Product] for [Audience]

**PMF Score: [X]/10 - [Ready / Mostly Ready / Needs Work / Misaligned]**

## Plain-English Answer

[Two or three short sentences. Say whether this is worth selling now, what is working, and what is broken.]

## What to Fix

[List up to three fixes. Only include fixes that matter.]

**[Fix 1]**
- What is wrong: [Simple sentence.]
- Fix it by: [Specific action.]

**[Fix 2]**
- What is wrong: [Simple sentence.]
- Fix it by: [Specific action.]

**[Fix 3]**
- What is wrong: [Simple sentence.]
- Fix it by: [Specific action.]
```
